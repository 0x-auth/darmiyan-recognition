The tower of orders

Time at one level becomes the position at the next. Each number line sits on the one below it.

What the tower shows

  • Each level is the time-axis of the one below. You read a base t, its time Δt = t + 1/t, and then that Δt is the base you stand on one floor up.
  • The floor is always 2. Every level has the same smallest tick, Δt ≥ 2, but its origin sits higher than the last. Rest climbs 1 → 2 → 2.5 → 2.9 → …
  • A fresh floor's own space is 3/2. Standing at a level's origin (Δt = 2), its space Δx = 2 − 1/2 = 3/2 — the dimension ceiling, at every level.
  • φ is the same step everywhere. Wherever the base is φ, Δt = √5 and Δx = 1.
  • The tower never closes. L(t) = t + 1/t has no fixed point (it would need 1/t = 0), so the climb goes on forever, the base growing like √(2k). The stack of orders is itself a self-reference that can't settle — the same reason time can't stop.
  • Within one level: the sequence (how many ticks) is 2ⁿ, the position (how many digits) is n. Climbing a level is zooming out until that time becomes the new position.
